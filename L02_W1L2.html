Think of the board as a city. The CPU is the brain in the middle. RAM is its desk: fast, but cleared when the power goes. The SSD is the filing cabinet: slower, but it remembers. The PSU is the power station. The chipset is the traffic cop for everything slower than RAM. Cables and slots are the roads.

Example 1. You open a photo. It is read from the SSD (cabinet) into RAM (desk) so the CPU (brain) can work on it fast.

Example 2. You add more RAM. The desk gets bigger, so more things can be open at once. The brain does not get faster.

Example 3. A Dell board is an odd shape with the front USB ports soldered on and a tiny 6-pin power plug. That is why, in Week 9, we need adapters to move it into a normal case.

Which part keeps your files when the power is off?

RAMCPUSSD / hard driveChipset
RAM is wiped at power-off; storage (SSD/HDD) is permanent.

The small plug near the CPU with a '4' on our map carries…

USB12 V just for the CPUvideonetwork
The 4-pin feeds 12 V to the CPU's voltage regulators.

Why does Dell's board shape matter for Week 9?

It is too heavyIts holes and ports do not match standard casesIt has no RAMIt cannot run Windows
Proprietary shape = adapters and an open frame instead of a normal case.

Words

Motherboard
The big board everything plugs into.
CPU
Central processing unit: the processor.
RAM
Random access memory: fast, temporary working memory.
PSU
Power supply unit.
Header
A row of pins on the board that a small plug fits onto.
Chipset
A chip that manages traffic between the CPU and slower parts.
