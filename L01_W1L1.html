Every PC you will touch this year is the same model: a Dell OptiPlex 7050 small form factor. That is good news. Learn one machine deeply and you can work on any of them.

Three rules apply every single lesson:

  1. Unplug before you open. Power off, pull the mains lead, then press the power button once to drain the last of the charge.
  2. Strap on before you touch. Your body can carry thousands of volts of static. You will not feel it; a RAM chip will.
  3. Check before you power on. The Navigator reads every connector, then calls the teacher. Only then does anyone press the button.

Example 1. Jess walks across the carpet in sneakers, sits down and picks up a stick of RAM. Nothing happens that she can see. The RAM fails two weeks later. ESD damage is often invisible and delayed.

Example 2. Sam straps on, clips to the painted part of the case, and feels safe. Paint is an insulator: the strap is not doing anything. Clip to bare metal — a screw head or the unpainted chassis edge.

Example 3. Ali is not sure a cable is plugged in all the way, powers on anyway, and the fan jams. "Not sure" always means stop and ask.

Why do you press the power button after unplugging?

To turn the fans onTo drain leftover charge from the boardTo reset the BIOSIt does nothing
Capacitors keep a little charge; pressing the button uses it up so the board is truly dead.

Where must the wrist-strap clip go?

Any part of the caseBare, unpainted metalThe plastic frontThe power cable
Paint and plastic are insulators. The strap only works through a metal path.

You are not sure if a plug is fully in. What now?

Power on and seePush harderStop, ask the Navigator or teacherPull it out and leave it
'Not sure' means stop. A loose plug can arc or jam a fan.
