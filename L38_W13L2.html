Crimp tool and cuttersThe crimp tool's blade and the flush cutters are sharp. Cut away from your hand, keep the tool closed on the bench, and never crimp a plug with a finger inside the jaws.
Lab cabling rulePatch leads go between bench PCs and the lab switch only. Never into a school wall port or patch panel.

4 minStartwhole class

  • Safety glasses on. Tools laid out: crimper, cutters, card, plugs, cable.
  • Teacher does one end under the camera, narrating the six steps.

8 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Six steps, same every time. Rushing step 3 (order) or step 5 (insert all the way) causes almost every failed cable. Click through the steps below before you start, then follow them at the bench.

Example 1. Wires cut at a slant: pins 1 and 2 reach the end of the plug, pins 7 and 8 stop short. The tester shows 7 and 8 open. Cut off, start again.

Example 2. Jacket not under the strain-relief bar: the lead works on the bench, then fails the first time someone trips on it.

Example 3. Untwisted 40 mm back to make the order easier: the lead passes a basic tester but drops gigabit link randomly (crosstalk). Keep the twist within 13 mm.

On the bench

  • 2 m Cat5e per pair (plus spare)
  • RJ45 plugs (at least 6 per pair: you will waste some)
  • Crimp tool with stripper and cutter
  • Flush cutters
  • Pinout card
  • Safety glasses

Words

Strain relief
The bar in the plug that clamps the jacket so pulling the cable does not pull the wires.
Insulation displacement
How the gold pins cut through the wire's insulation when crimped.
Tug test
A firm pull on the cable after crimping; nothing should move.
Boot
Optional rubber cover that protects the clip; it goes on BEFORE the plug.
