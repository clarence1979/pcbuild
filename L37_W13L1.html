Eight wires, one correct order. T568B is the common standard in Australia and the one we use on both ends. Memorise it as four pairs: orange, green, blue, brown, with the striped (white) wire of each pair first, except that the blue pair sits in the middle and splits the green pair.

Example 1 — 100 Mb/s. Fast Ethernet uses only pins 1,2 (send) and 3,6 (receive). The blue and brown pairs are spare. That is why a cable with a broken blue wire can still 'work' at 100 Mb/s and then fail at gigabit.

Example 2 — 1 Gb/s. Gigabit uses all four pairs, in both directions at once. All eight wires must be right.

Example 3 — A on one end, B on the other. That is a crossover cable. Old gear needed it to connect PC to PC. Modern NICs swap automatically, so the cable works, but anyone testing it would call it a miswire. Both ends B, always.

Words

T568B
The wiring standard we use: white-orange, orange, white-green, blue, white-blue, green, white-brown, brown.
T568A
The other standard; same wires, orange and green pairs swapped.
Pin 1
The leftmost pin when the clip is down and the gold pins face you.
Straight-through
Same standard on both ends; what every lab lead is.
