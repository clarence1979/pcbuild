By the end of this lesson you can

  1. Apply the bottom-up ladder to find a network fault in under five minutes.
  2. Use ping, ipconfig/ip a, arp, and the link light as evidence.
  3. Log each fault with the rung it failed on.
Driver: hands on the hardwareNavigator: reads each step aloud, ticks it off, checks before power-onSwap roles every lesson
Lab cabling rulePatch leads go between bench PCs and the lab switch only. Never into a school wall port or patch panel.

3 minStartwhole class

  • Rotate benches. Each PC has one planted network fault. Log sheet ready.
  • Teacher: 'Do not touch a setting until you have named the rung.'

6 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Possible plants: lead unplugged at the switch · lead with an open wire · wrong mask (/16 or /25) · wrong third octet · duplicate IP with another bench · firewall blocking ICMP · IPv4 disabled on the adapter · switch port moved. Each one fails at a specific rung. Find the rung, then the cause.

Example. Rung 1 OK (light on). Rung 2: ip a shows 169.254.7.3. The PC is waiting for DHCP: the static setting was removed. Fix the setting; everything above comes back.

Words

Bottom-up
Start at the cable and work up: link, address, loopback, self, neighbour, application.
Duplicate IP
Two PCs with the same address; both get intermittent failures.
APIPA
169.254.x.x: the address a PC gives itself when it expected DHCP and got nothing.
