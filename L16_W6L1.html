14 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

The wall gives 230 V AC. Chips want 1–12 V DC. The PSU converts, filters and splits that into three rails: 12 V (CPU, GPU, fans, motors), 5 V (USB, drives, logic) and 3.3 V (some chips). Each cable carries a mix of these plus ground.

Example 1 — watts. Our upgraded build: CPU 65 W + board 25 W + GPU 130 W + drives and fans 20 W = 240 W at full tilt. Add 30% headroom = 312 W. A 450–550 W PSU is plenty; the Dell 180 W is not.

Example 2 — connectors. 24-pin main, 4+4 (8-pin) CPU, 6+2 PCIe for graphics, flat 15-pin SATA power. All keyed; none fit the wrong socket.

Example 3 — Dell. The 7050 board has no 24-pin socket: it uses a 6-pin main plug plus the 4-pin CPU plug. A 24-to-6 adapter made for the 3050/5050/7050 solves it. The 4-pin CPU plug is standard.

Words

Rail
One of the PSU's output voltages: 3.3 V, 5 V or 12 V.
Watt
Power = volts × amps. A 550 W PSU can deliver up to 550 W total.
80 Plus
An efficiency rating: Bronze, Gold, Platinum.
PS_ON
The green wire; the board pulls it low to switch the PSU on.
PWR_OK
The grey wire; the PSU says 'voltages are stable' so the CPU can start.
