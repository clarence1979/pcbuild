A 65 W processor is a 65 W heater the size of a postage stamp. Without a cooler it reaches 100 °C in seconds and shuts itself down.

Example 1 — the socket. On LGA1151 the 1151 spring pins live in the socket. One bent pin can kill the board. That is why the chip is lowered straight down with the triangle matched, and the lever does the clamping, not your thumb.

Example 2 — paste. Two metal surfaces look flat but are rough under a microscope. Air in those gaps is an insulator. Paste pushes the air out. A pea-sized dot in the centre is enough; the cooler's pressure spreads it.

Example 3 — too much. Half a tube squeezes out the sides, onto the socket, and makes a mess that is hard to clean. More is worse, not better.

Words

LGA
Land grid array: the pins are in the socket, the chip has flat pads.
Heat spreader
The metal lid on top of the CPU.
Thermal paste
A grey compound that fills gaps between CPU and cooler so heat can cross.
Heatsink
The finned metal block that spreads heat into the air.
