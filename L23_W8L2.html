Before a transplant, a technician proves fit on paper: hole positions, port positions, power plugs, button plug, card bracket. Every mismatch gets a plan: an adapter, a washer, an empty hole, a riser. Only then does the build start.

Example 1. Nine holes on the board; six line up with the frame's slotted rails. Six standoffs is enough support. The three odd holes get nothing: a standoff under a hole with no screw is fine, a standoff touching a solder pad is not.

Example 2. The front USB block is soldered to the board edge. On an open frame it is reachable, so it still works; in a closed case it would be buried.

Example 3. The GPU bracket sits 4 mm off the frame's bracket rail because the Dell slot is not where ATX expects. A short riser cable fixes it; or on an open frame, the card simply hangs in the slot supported by the latch, with no screw.

Words

Hole pattern
Where the board's mounting holes are, measured from one corner.
Mismatch
A board hole with no matching frame hole: leave it without a standoff.
Riser
A short flexible PCIe extension used when a card's bracket does not line up.
Audit
A checklist that proves every part will work together before you start.
