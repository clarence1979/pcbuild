10 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Without a router or DHCP server, nothing will give your PC an address. You set it yourself: address 192.168.10.(10 + bench), mask 255.255.255.0, gateway empty. Then you prove each layer from the bottom: link light (cable), ip a (address set), ping 127.0.0.1 (stack works), ping your own address (NIC works).

Example 1. Link light off: it is never the IP settings. Cable, port, or NIC.

Example 2. Link light on, ping 127.0.0.1 fails: the OS's TCP/IP stack is broken or the firewall is blocking even itself. Rare; reboot first.

Example 3. Everything local passes but a neighbour does not answer: that is next lesson. Stop at your own address today.

Words

Static IP
An address you type in, which never changes.
DHCP
A service that hands out addresses automatically; we have none, so we go static.
Link light
The LED on the NIC and switch port: lit = cable and both ends are electrically talking.
Loopback
127.0.0.1: the address that means 'this PC'.
