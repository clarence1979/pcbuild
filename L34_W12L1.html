A LAN is computers in one room (or building) joined by cables to a switch. Each computer's network port is a NIC with a unique MAC address, like a serial number. Data goes in chunks called frames; each frame has a 'to' MAC and a 'from' MAC. The switch reads the 'from' on every frame and learns which port each MAC lives on. After that, it sends frames to the right port only.

Example 1 — hub vs switch. An old hub repeated every frame to every port, like shouting in a room. A switch is like passing a note to the right person.

Example 2 — router. The switch joins PCs on one network. A router joins networks: your LAN to your ISP's network, and on to the internet. Our lab has no router: it is an island on purpose.

Example 3 — MAC. 3C-7C-3F-11-AA-02: the first half says who made the chip, the second half is the unit. Change the network card and the MAC changes with it.

Words

LAN
Local area network: devices in one place joined together.
NIC
Network interface card: the Ethernet port and its chip.
MAC address
A 48-bit hardware address burnt into every NIC, written as 12 hex characters.
Switch
A box that learns which MAC is on which port and sends frames only where needed.
Router
Joins different networks together, e.g. your LAN to the internet.
