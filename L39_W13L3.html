10 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

A basic tester sends a pulse down each wire in turn. The remote unit lights the pin it arrives on. A good straight-through lead lights 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8 in order on both units. Anything else tells you what went wrong and, usually, which end.

Example 1 — open. Master lights 4, remote shows nothing for 4. Wire 4 did not reach its pin on one end. Look at both plugs; the short wire is visible through the plastic.

Example 2 — miswire. Master 2, remote 3; master 3, remote 2. Green and orange swapped on one end. Cut that end off.

Example 3 — split pair. Everything lights 1→8 in order, but you used white-blue on pin 3 and white-green on pin 5. The tester cannot tell. The cable links but drops out at gigabit. Only a visual check or a certifier catches it, so the Navigator check before crimping matters.

Words

Open
A wire not connected at one end: that number never lights at the remote.
Short
Two wires touching: two lights at once.
Miswire
Wrong order: the remote lights in a different sequence.
Split pair
Right pins, wrong pairing (e.g. 3 and 5 swapped): passes a basic tester, fails at speed.
Continuity
An unbroken electrical path.
