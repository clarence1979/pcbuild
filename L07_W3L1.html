RAM is the CPU's desk. It holds whatever is open right now. It is about 100 times faster than an SSD, but it only works while powered: cut the power and the desk is wiped. That is why you 'save' to the SSD.

Reading a label. 8GB 1Rx8 PC4-2400T-UA means 8 GB capacity, single rank, DDR4, 2400 MT/s, unbuffered (desktop). Simpler labels say DDR4-2400 8GB UDIMM.

Example 1 — capacity. 8 GB runs Windows and a browser. 16 GB is comfortable for games and editing. 32 GB is for heavy work. More than you use is wasted money.

Example 2 — speed. The i5-7500 runs memory at 2400 MT/s. Put in 3200 sticks and they slow down to 2400. Nothing breaks; you just paid for speed you cannot use.

Example 3 — mixing. Two different 8 GB sticks usually work, but at the slower speed. A DDR3 stick will not even go in: the notch is in a different place.

Words

DIMM
Dual in-line memory module: the stick that goes in a desktop.
SO-DIMM
The short laptop version. Does not fit a desktop slot.
DDR4
The generation. DDR3, DDR4 and DDR5 all have different notches and do not mix.
MT/s
Mega-transfers per second: the speed on the label, e.g. 2400.
Dual channel
Two sticks working as a pair so data moves on two lanes at once.
