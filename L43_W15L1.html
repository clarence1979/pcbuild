8 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Twenty PCs, one switch, one network. Each PC already has its address. Now they find each other. When you ping .12, your PC first broadcasts an ARP request ('who has 192.168.10.12?'). PC-02 replies with its MAC. The switch learns both ports. Then the ping travels directly. Watch it happen below, then do it for real.

Example 1. Ping a neighbour: 'Request timed out' on all four. Their link light is on and their address is right. Their firewall is dropping ICMP. Allow it (Windows: enable 'File and Printer Sharing (Echo Request – ICMPv4-In)'; Mint: ufw allow from 192.168.10.0/24, or ufw is off by default).

Example 2. 'Destination host unreachable' from your OWN address: the destination is on a different network or does not exist; ARP got no reply.

Example 3. arp -a shows the neighbour's IP next to a MAC that matches their label. That is the proof the two layers are linked.

Words

Port plan
A table: switch port → bench → IP → MAC.
ARP
Address Resolution Protocol: 'who has this IP? tell me your MAC'.
ICMP
The protocol ping uses: echo request and echo reply.
Firewall
Software on each PC that blocks unwanted incoming traffic; by default it often blocks ping.
