12 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

A power button is just a momentary switch: press it and two pins touch for a moment. The board sees that and starts the PSU. Standard boards put those pins in a 2×5 block. Dell runs the button, LED and front USB through its own plugs instead.

Example 1. A standard case: the 'PWR SW' two-pin plug goes on the PWR SW pins, either way round. The 'PWR LED' plug must go + to +, or the light never comes on (nothing breaks, it just stays dark).

Example 2. Our transplant: the open frame comes with a standard two-pin switch. The Dell board wants its 5-pin plug. Either we use a Dell-to-standard adapter, or we unscrew Dell's own button module from the SFF case and run it on its original cable.

Example 3. The 'screwdriver start': with no button at all, a tech briefly touches the two PWR SW pins with a screwdriver tip. It works because a switch is just a short. We will not need it, but you should know what it means.

A power switch plug is connected backwards. Result:

The PC will not turn onWorks fine: switches have no polarityThe LED breaksThe PSU is damaged
A switch just connects two pins.

The power LED stays dark after a build. Likely cause:

Dead PSULED plug reversedNo RAMWrong case
LEDs only pass current one way.

Why does the Dell board need special treatment for the button?

It has no buttonIt uses a 5-pin plug instead of the standard 2×5 headerIt needs 12 VIt uses a wireless button
Proprietary plug → adapter or reuse Dell's own module.

Words

Front-panel header
The pin block for power button, reset, power LED, drive LED.
Polarity
Which way round + and − go; matters for LEDs, not switches.
Momentary switch
A button that only connects while pressed; the board does the rest.
