14 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Three generations of storage are still sold today. A hard drive stores bits as magnetism on spinning discs; the arm has to physically move to find data. A SATA SSD stores bits in flash chips with no motion, but talks over the old SATA cable (about 550 MB/s max). An NVMe SSD uses the same flash but plugs straight into PCIe lanes, so it can move several gigabytes a second.

Example 1 — boot time. Windows on HDD: 60–90 s. On SATA SSD: 15 s. On NVMe: 10 s. The jump from HDD to any SSD is the one you feel most.

Example 2 — capacity per dollar. HDDs are still the cheapest per terabyte, so big video archives often stay on HDD.

Example 3 — keying. An M.2 slot with an 'M key' notch takes NVMe (and on this board, SATA M.2 too). A 'B+M' key SATA-only stick fits an M-key slot physically but may not work in an NVMe-only slot. Read the spec, not just the shape.

Words

HDD
Hard disk drive: spinning magnetic platters and a moving arm.
SSD
Solid state drive: flash memory chips, no moving parts.
NVMe
The fast protocol SSDs use over PCIe lanes.
M.2
The small stick-shaped slot; 2280 = 22 mm wide, 80 mm long.
TBW
Terabytes written: how much an SSD can write in its life.
