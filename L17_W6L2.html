12 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Sizing a PSU is arithmetic. List every part, write its peak watts, add them, then add about 30% so the PSU is not running flat out (quieter, cooler, longer life). Round up to a size that exists.

Example 1. i5-7500 (65) + board and RAM (25) + NVMe (7) + two fans (6) + GTX 1650 (75) = 178 W. ×1.3 = 231 W. A 450 W unit is already generous.

Example 2. Same build with an RTX 3060 (170 W): 273 W ×1.3 = 355 W. Choose 550 W.

Example 3. A budget '600 W' no-name unit with a 12 V rail rated 20 A can only really deliver 240 W where it matters. Read the 12 V line, not the big number on the box.

Try it: change the parts and watch the gauge.

Words

Headroom
Spare capacity above the expected peak, usually 25–30%.
BIST
Built-in self-test: the small button and LED on the back of a Dell PSU.
PSU tester
A box that fakes the motherboard so the PSU turns on and shows each rail's voltage.
Peak draw
The most the build will pull with everything busy.
