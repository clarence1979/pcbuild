8 minLearnteacher explains, you follow on screen

Two installs today, both low-force. The M.2 stick slides in at an angle and is pinned flat with one screw. The SATA drive sits in a caddy and gets two cables: thin L-shaped data to the board, wider flat power from the PSU.

Example 1. Pressing the M.2 flat before it is fully in the slot: the gold fingers are only half in, the screw goes in, and the drive is never detected. Push in first, then press down.

Example 2. The M.2 screw is tiny and the tray is not magnetic; it rolls under the PSU. Use the magnetic tray and the small driver, nothing else.

Example 3. SATA data plugged in, power forgotten: the BIOS sees nothing. Two cables, always.

The M.2 drive is screwed down but not detected. Most likely…

the screw is too tightit was pressed flat before it was fully in the slotthe OS is missingthe SATA cable is loose
Half-inserted fingers = no contact. Remove, re-insert at 30°, then press down.

A SATA drive needs…

one cable: datatwo cables: data and powerthree cablesno cables, it is M.2
Data from the board, power from the PSU.

The L-shaped plug will not go in. You should…

push harderflip it: the L matches the port one way onlyuse a different drivebend the pins
The L-shape keys the orientation.

Words

Retaining screw
The single tiny screw holding down the M.2 stick.
Standoff
The raised threaded post the M.2 screw goes into.
SATA data cable
The thin 7-pin L-shaped cable; data only.
SATA power
The wider 15-pin flat plug from the PSU.
